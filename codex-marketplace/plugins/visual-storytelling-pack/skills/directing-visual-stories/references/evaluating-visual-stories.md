# Evaluating visual stories

Evaluation asks whether the intended audience change survives the chosen medium.
It is a finite review of evidence, not a popularity vote or a request for generic
polish. Run only the checks relevant to the output: a frame, complete field,
sequence, page, or interactive world.

## Finite review

Record `pass`, `revise`, or `blocked` for each applicable check, with one concrete
observation and the next correction. A direction is not ready when a required
decision is merely implied.

1. **Story:** Can a reviewer state the intended audience change and decisive beat
   without adding invented context?
2. **Hierarchy:** At thumbnail or reduced-detail scale, is the first read clear,
   and does the second/final read reward attention rather than compete immediately?
3. **Frame:** Do crop, scale, viewpoint, perspective, aspect, negative space, and
   off-frame pressure express the beat? Is the containing field also accounted for?
4. **Depth and attention:** Do planes, focus, contrast, gaze, vectors, and detail
   density lead the intended path? Is any occlusion fair to the audience?
5. **Light and colour:** Is the source or role motivated? Do contrast and palette
   reveal, conceal, separate, or mark state rather than merely decorate?
6. **Field and rhythm:** Does the page, spread, stage, screen, or world have entry,
   dwell, transition, and exit? Does every subordinate frame earn its place?
7. **Sequence or agency:** Where time or choice exists, is the before/after state,
   route, threshold, consequence, and recovery legible? Can alternate viewpoints
   still find the landmark or story evidence?
8. **Truth and custody:** Are copy, canon, references, and provenance supported?
   Are canonical works used as function anchors rather than imitation prompts, and
   are output/custody claims honest?
9. **Translation:** Did the downstream medium preserve the declared hierarchy,
   story job, and invariants? Record what was changed by crop, viewport, state,
   player agency, reduced motion, or provider limits.

## Evidence by output

- **Frame/image:** inspect the full frame and a thumbnail; test the intended crop,
  focal read, invariant, and any edit boundary. The image-generation handoff must
  retain its translated prompt and actual result destination.
- **Page/complete field:** inspect the whole page before local modules, then the
  smallest viewport and a zoomed/reduced-detail view. Check field order, text
  spatial role, meaningful intervals, and the final field.
- **Sequence:** inspect the beat map as stills and in time. Confirm each transition
  changes knowledge, feeling, expectation, or state, and that stillness has a job.
- **Interactive world:** inspect arrival, alternate viewpoint, threshold,
  interaction, consequence, and return. Record the route taken and whether a
  participant can recover orientation after dwelling or wandering.

## Terminal states

`pass` means the evidence supports the direction at the intended scale and medium.
`revise` means the story is recoverable with a bounded composition change. `blocked`
means a required fact, asset, capability, or human-owned creative decision is
missing; do not generate or implement around it. Keep rejected alternatives and
the accepted rationale in the brief trace or field-trial note.

### Avoid

- replacing an audience-change review with “looks good”;
- checking only the hero frame and ignoring the containing field or route;
- treating technical validity, accessibility, or provider success as story proof;
- using a checklist to force a single style or to erase productive ambiguity.
