# Lighting, colour, and selective visibility

Light is a composition and information system. Brief what the audience should be
able to see, what remains uncertain, and what changes when the light changes.

## Specify the source and behaviour

Name the motivated or deliberately unreal source, its direction and height, quality
(hard, soft, diffuse, broken), intensity relationship, falloff, shadow behaviour,
and affected planes. The source may be a window, practical, sun, screen, fire,
stage cue, reflected surface, or an authored graphic field. “Cinematic lighting”
and “moody light” are not actionable until translated into these choices.

- Front light clarifies and can reduce modelling.
- Side light models texture, separation, conflict, and material difference.
- Back light separates silhouette, hides identity, or makes a threshold readable.
- High/low light alters naturalism, status, danger, and bodily emphasis.
- Hard/soft edges decide whether a shape is cut out, joined, fragile, or calm.
- Falloff and shadow connect or separate planes and control where attention rests.

Use darkness, glare, colour absence, or blocked light to withhold information,
delay a reveal, protect a secret, simplify a field, or make the audience search.
Selective visibility must serve a beat; it must not merely lower image quality.

## Give colour a role

Assign colours by relationship rather than adjective:

- establish the world or time/place;
- separate subject from environment;
- identify a faction, object, or recurring motif;
- indicate emotional or systemic pressure;
- mark state change, threshold, or consequence;
- reserve an exception for the active beat.

State which colours are stable and which can change. A palette rupture can be a
turning point; a restrained palette can make one warm or bright signal carry the
story. “Teal and orange,” “neon,” or “dark mode” are surfaces, not intentions.

## Translate without losing the story

For an image, describe the physical light and its visible consequence. For a page
or world, describe luminance fields, colour roles, selective revelation, shadow,
translucency, and state cues; implementation owns contrast mechanics and
accessibility verification. For an interactive field, define which cue changes on
entry, action, time, or state, and how the participant knows what changed.

## Check

At intended size and in a reduced-detail view, identify the first lit/coloured
subject, the withheld region, and the state cue. If a colour is the only carrier of
meaning, record a non-colour cue for downstream accessibility or interaction work.
Do not let atmospheric treatment erase evidence, hierarchy, or text legibility.
