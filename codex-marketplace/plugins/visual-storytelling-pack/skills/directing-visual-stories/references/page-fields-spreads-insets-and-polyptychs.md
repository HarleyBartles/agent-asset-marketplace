# Page fields, spreads, insets, and polyptychs

Use this route when the story is carried by a complete field rather than by one
isolated image. A page, spread, full viewport, long scroll passage, or composed
screen can be one frame in its own right; it can also contain frames that carry
smaller beats. The question is not how to decorate a grid. It is how the whole
field controls entry, emphasis, dwell, interruption, and exit.

## The super-composition

Start with the dominant field: the image, colour mass, spatial claim, or silence
that establishes the audience's first condition. Place subordinate panels,
captions, details, and evidence in relation to that field. Each subordinate unit
must either advance the beat, qualify it, or create a productive counter-read.
If a panel could be removed without changing the audience's understanding or
rhythm, it is probably ornamental coverage.

- **Dominant field:** establishes place, scale, emotional weather, or the central
  proposition. It may be a full-bleed image, matte, landscape, diagram, tableau,
  or deliberately empty zone.
- **Inset:** narrows time or scale. Use it for a hand, eye, prop, reaction,
  memory, parallel action, or evidence that needs a different reading distance.
- **Gutter or interval:** delegates change, silence, delay, or inference. An
  interval is not wasted space; it controls how much work the audience performs.
- **Margin:** can hold annotation, chronology, provenance, secondary voices, or
  breathing room. It becomes part of the story when it changes the relation
  between main event and commentary.
- **Exit:** gives the eye or body a next move: a page turn, threshold, route,
  scroll continuation, interaction, or a held unresolved edge.

## Spread and full-page choices

A spread is one field with a centre, edges, and cross-field vectors. Decide what
can cross the centre and what must not be split. Place a subject near an edge when
the audience should feel pull, escape, or incompleteness; place it near the centre
when the binding or threshold should make the relationship feel held. A web page
can translate this as one authored viewport or scroll passage. Preserve the
dominant/subordinate relationship and the intended pause rather than copying
paper coordinates.

For a single full-page frame, still name its internal hierarchy: entry point,
secondary discovery, quiet zone, and implied next state. “One image” is not a
reason to abandon sequence thinking. The page can establish a place and contain
its own reveal through depth, light, off-frame pressure, or a detail that only
becomes legible after the first read.

## Insets, continuous narrative, and polyptychs

Use a super-panel or matte with insets when the audience needs both a stable world
and several incidents inside it. The large field supplies orientation or shared
emotional state; insets supply causally precise moments. Vary inset scale to vary
duration and importance. Keep a visible relation between inset and the field so
the panels do not become floating cards over wallpaper.

Use a polyptych when the frame should stay recognisably stable while time, state,
or absence changes. Repeated viewpoint makes difference visible: a person leaves,
weather turns, damage accumulates, a crowd thins, or a place is revisited. A
continuous narrative can place several moments in one environment; the repeated
subject and changed position make time spatial. Use this only when simultaneity
or comparison is the point, otherwise ordinary sequencing may be clearer.

## Rhythm and translation

Compose density as rhythm: large and small fields, full bleed and margin, image
and text, event and silence. A stable underlying grammar makes a rupture legible,
but variation should be earned by the beat. Responsive or interactive translation
must preserve roles and order of importance, not the literal desktop arrangement.
When the field changes across states, keep the landmark, hierarchy, and transition
invariant explicit; do not let a framework's stacking order invent the story.

Treat the grid as infrastructure: a stable column, baseline, or margin grammar
makes a deliberate rupture readable. Montage compresses separated events when
juxtaposition is the cause; do not use it when continuity is needed. Give
editorial voices—caption, annotation, transcript, image, or interface—distinct
spatial roles so commentary can agree with, complicate, or contradict the dominant
field. Typography is a spatial actor: scale, measure, density, and placement alter
tempo and authority. A page-turn analogue is any deliberate reveal, fold, scroll
threshold, or state change that withholds then releases evidence; name what changes
and what remains oriented.

### Route output

Record: dominant field; local frames and their story jobs; entry, dwell, and exit;
reading order or agency; interval/gutter meaning; what crosses boundaries; and
the invariant that survives viewport, state, or route changes. Then route each
local beat to crop, depth, light, attention, or detail references as needed.

### Avoid

- cards floating over a wallpaper image with no spatial or causal relationship;
- filling every module, margin, or empty zone;
- splitting a dominant subject at an arbitrary fold or breakpoint;
- using panel count as a substitute for pacing;
- treating an editorial, comic, or magazine surface as a style prompt rather than
  a story system.
