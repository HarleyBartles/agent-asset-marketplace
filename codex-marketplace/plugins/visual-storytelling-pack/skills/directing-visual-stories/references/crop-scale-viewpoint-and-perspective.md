# Crop, scale, viewpoint, and perspective

Crop and camera position are narrative verbs. Select them for the information and
relationship they make available, not because a shot-size label sounds polished.

## Choose distance and boundary

| Choice | Makes available | Risk or useful omission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| extreme wide / establishing | place, scale, isolation, route, relation | emotion and evidence become small |
| wide / full figure | body-in-space, blocking, environment | detail may compete with the beat |
| medium | exchange, behaviour, social distance | surroundings can lose causal context |
| close-up | reaction, thought, intimacy, pressure | context becomes inference |
| extreme close / insert | tactile evidence, identity, consequence, motif | can become ornamental coverage |

Treat a tight crop as an active exclusion. Hands, eyes, documents, wounds, tools,
or traces deserve the frame when their detail changes what the audience knows or
expects. Otherwise retain the wider relationship. A partial body or object at the
edge can imply a witness, threat, continuation, or unseen cause.

Negative space is also a boundary decision. Leave space when it holds a gaze,
movement, absence, waiting, copy, danger, or implied arrival. Remove it when
pressure, enclosure, or tactile immediacy is the point. Do not fill it merely to
avoid an “empty” image.

## Choose viewpoint and perspective

State where the audience stands: participant, witness, accomplice, observer,
surveillance position, or neutral survey. Then specify:

- camera height and tilt: power, vulnerability, equality, instability;
- distance and physical access: intimacy, social boundary, detachment;
- angle and orientation: alignment, opposition, discovery, concealment;
- perspective behaviour: converging lines, compression, exaggeration, or a flat
  graphic field;
- aspect ratio and boundary: what the short side pressures, hides, or lets breathe.

Perspective is not only lens jargon. A compressed space can make a distant threat
feel near; a wide perspective can expose a route while stretching relationships.
An overhead view can turn people into a pattern or reveal a plan; an eye-level
view can honour shared presence. Name the intended effect and let implementation
choose technically equivalent means.

## Composition brief

Write the choice in causal form: “Use a close crop from the witness's low,
oblique viewpoint so the marked hand becomes evidence while the unseen owner
remains uncertain; retain a narrow exit of negative space toward the off-frame
sound.” Include the containing field and the next read. Avoid “dramatic angle,”
“dynamic crop,” or “cinematic perspective” without a story consequence.

## Check

Shrink the frame or view it at intended delivery size. Can the audience identify
the beat and the direction of implied space? If not, widen, simplify, or strengthen
the evidence before adding detail.
