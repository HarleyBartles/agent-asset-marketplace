# Staging, blocking, and audience relationship

Use this route when meaning depends on where bodies, objects, content regions, or
landmarks stand in relation to one another and to the audience. Theatre makes the
principle explicit: a stage picture can tell the truth of a relationship before
anyone speaks. The transferable tool is spatial authorship, not curtains,
spotlights, or a theatrical skin.

## Compose relationships, not occupants

Name the relationship the audience must read: alliance, exclusion, threat,
dependence, status, intimacy, witness, or isolation. Then choose the spatial
evidence:

- **Proximity** can express intimacy, alliance, threat, or enforced closeness.
  Distance can express exclusion, status, grief, or an obstacle that words deny.
- **Levels** change power and attention. Height, depth, elevation, and enclosure
  can make a figure dominant, exposed, protected, or overlooked.
- **Gaze and orientation** tell the audience who is attending to whom, who refuses
  contact, who watches unseen, and where an absent subject is implied.
- **Grouping and negative space** show coalition, fracture, waiting, or a missing
  participant. Empty space can be the place a relationship cannot cross.
- **Obstruction and territory** externalise subtext. A desk, doorway, road, panel
  edge, HUD region, or landmark can divide people while keeping the division
  visible.

Blocking is a sequence of changing relationships. Approach, withdrawal, crossing,
turning away, touch, interruption, entrance, and exit each redraw the field. A
movement should therefore have a story verb—join, confront, evade, reveal, pursue,
yield, or abandon—not merely “add energy.” A held tableau can be more informative
than motion when the audience needs to read a social truth or anticipate a break.

## Audience position

The audience contract changes what staging must provide:

- **Contained/proscenium:** the author can compose one privileged view and use
  precise reveal, concealment, and sightline.
- **Exposed/thrust or in-the-round:** the relationship must survive several sides;
  back-facing bodies, occlusion, and asymmetry become part of the audience's
  negotiated view.
- **Promenade/exploratory:** route and discovery are composition. Thresholds,
  landmarks, orientation cues, and recoverable views matter more than one perfect
  tableau.
- **Direct address:** the work collapses observer distance. Eye contact, exposed
  interface elements, or a subject looking out of the frame can make the audience
  a participant, witness, accomplice, or target.

For a page or interface, treat content blocks, controls, images, and empty zones as
staged participants. For an interactive world, test arrival, dwell, interaction,
return, and departure; each can rearrange the relationship between player and
place. For a tile-based town, roads, thresholds, building footprints, NPC facing,
and landmark distance are blocking in a persistent field.

## Cues and transitions

An entrance, light change, sound, camera move, scroll threshold, hover, state
change, or player action is a cue. Coordinate cues when the audience should feel a
dramatic threshold; withhold or stagger them when uncertainty is the point. A
cue that changes attention without changing the story is decoration. A static
composition should still imply what would cause the next entrance, crossing, or
reconfiguration.

### Route output

Record: relationship and audience role; subject/object positions; proximity,
levels, gaze, territory, and empty zones; likely audience viewpoints; movement or
cue verbs; and the change in relation at the next beat. State which relationships
must remain readable from alternate routes or viewport arrangements.

### Avoid

- placing figures for visual balance while ignoring their relationship;
- using theatrical décor, a spotlight, or “stagey” language as the direction;
- choreographing movement without a change in knowledge, feeling, or agency;
- designing one screenshot when the audience can move around the field;
- letting interface chrome or decorative animation contradict the staged priority.
