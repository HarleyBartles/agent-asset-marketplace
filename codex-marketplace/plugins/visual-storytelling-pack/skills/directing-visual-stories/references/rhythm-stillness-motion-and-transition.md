# Rhythm, stillness, motion, and transition

Use this route when the story depends on when attention changes, not only where
elements sit. Rhythm is the pattern of duration, density, repetition, interruption,
and release across frames, panels, page fields, scroll, animation, cues, or player
movement. Motion is one instrument in that pattern; it is not a default quality
signal.

## Build a beat map

Name the sequence of audience changes before choosing effects:

1. establish or orient;
2. hold, invite search, or create anticipation;
3. interrupt or reveal;
4. register reaction or consequence;
5. release, resolve, or leave a changed expectation.

The order may differ, but every transition should have a verb such as reveal,
transfer, pursue, retreat, search, collide, remember, or return. Record the
before-state and after-state. If they are indistinguishable, the motion is likely
ornamental.

## Stillness is active

A held image, empty field, repeated tableau, quiet scroll interval, or unmoving
background can create contemplation, scale, suspense, aftermath, or the sense of
time passing. Stillness gives the next change contrast. In anime and limited
animation, effort is allocated to decisive poses, contact, anticipation, impact,
reaction, environmental movement, camera change, or light rather than evenly
animating every layer. Borrow that allocation logic, not a studio's surface style.

In a page, a large quiet field can arrest the reader after dense evidence. In a
game, a calm traversal or stable landmark lets the player recover orientation. In
an image brief, a held pose or static environment can make a small light shift or
gesture carry the beat.

## Motion hierarchy

Choose the layer that should move and hold the rest where possible:

- **Subject motion:** intention, action, impact, or emotional change.
- **Camera/view motion:** reveal, pursuit, retreat, search, destabilisation, or
  transfer of point of view.
- **Environment motion:** weather, crowd, machinery, water, foliage, signage, or
  atmospheric time.
- **Light/colour motion:** state cue, attention transfer, time shift, danger, or
  selective disclosure.
- **Interface/world transition:** threshold, route change, feedback, or changed
  condition.

One moving layer against still support often directs attention better than motion
everywhere. A sequence can change colour, crop, focus, or density instead of
position. A transition should preserve the audience's orientation unless
disorientation is the intended beat and recovery is designed afterward.

## Anime layout and compositing as a briefing model

Treat each shot or state as a layout contract: camera, background, subject pose,
depth, effects, lighting, timing, and finish are one decision. Key poses and
silhouettes should make intention legible at the intended reading distance.
Compositing unifies layers so that character, environment, effects, and light
belong to one world. Graphic deformation, speed abstraction, or palette rupture
should mark force or subjectivity, not become an all-purpose filter.

## Sequence in interactive work

Player-controlled pace changes the rhythm contract. Provide designed thresholds,
landmarks, and feedback, but allow a player to dwell without destroying hierarchy.
For exploratory worlds, rhythm includes arrival, search, interaction, consequence,
return, and departure. Revisit should reveal changed state rather than replay an
unchanged tableau. For a tile-based town, alternate quiet streets, social nodes,
landmark vistas, and event spaces; do not make every tile animate or every route
announce itself at maximum intensity.

### Route output

Record: beat order; before/after state; duration or dwell intent; motion budget by
layer; the stillness that gives change contrast; trigger/cue; orientation and
recovery rule; and the invariant that survives reduced motion, slower reading, or
player-controlled pace.

### Avoid

- motion whose only rationale is “dynamic” or “cinematic”;
- constant maximum intensity, camera movement, parallax, or hover effects;
- animating all layers equally so no subject is active;
- using a transition to hide a weak composition;
- treating anime as a visual filter instead of a layout, pose, timing, and
  compositing discipline.
