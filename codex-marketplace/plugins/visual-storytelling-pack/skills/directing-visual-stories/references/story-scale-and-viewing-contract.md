# Story scale and viewing contract

Composition is nested. Decide which scale carries the beat, then check the
containing field before treating a local frame as successful:

`object -> frame -> complete field -> sequence`

- **Object** is a detail, prop, face, hand, tile, word, or patch of light. It
  carries evidence or a motif.
- **Frame** is a bounded image, shot, panel, inset, viewport, or screen region.
  It controls a moment and a point of view.
- **Complete field** is the whole image, page, spread, stage picture, screen,
  town, or navigable scene. It controls relationships among local frames.
- **Sequence** is change across panels, cuts, scroll, cues, animation, movement,
  state, or revisitation. It controls before/after and expectation.

Name the smallest scale at which the audience can understand the intended change,
and the larger scale that gives it context. A hand may be the frame's subject,
but the full image must still explain whose hand it is, what it touches, and what
the crop makes us infer. A panel may be legible alone, but its page position can
change its timing, emphasis, or relation to the next panel.

## Viewing contracts

The audience may control different parts of the read:

| Contract | Author controls | Composition must provide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `directed` | view, order, and often timing | precise reveal, crop, focus transfer, and beat |
| `guided` | main route and hierarchy; audience controls pace | landmarks, thresholds, rhythm, and hierarchy that survive dwelling |
| `exploratory` | rules and affordances; audience chooses view/order | readable landmarks, multiple viable observation points, state evidence, and orientation |

Do not import directed-film precision into an exploratory world without a recovery
plan. Do not flatten a directed image into a collection of equally available
details. The contract is a creative constraint: it determines where ambiguity is
useful and where the work must explain itself.

## Route questions

Before choosing a composition family, answer:

1. What should change in the audience: knowledge, feeling, anticipation,
   orientation, relationship, or agency?
2. What is the beat at object, frame, field, and sequence scales?
3. What is shown, withheld, delayed, or left off-frame?
4. Who owns the view, and can the audience look elsewhere or return later?
5. What must survive a crop, viewport change, state change, or player route?

If the answer is only “make it cinematic/comic/editorial,” keep the direction
open and route by story function. Medium labels are not composition decisions.

## Output implication

Record `scale`, `viewing contract`, `audience change`, `beat`, `containing field`,
and `survival invariant`. Then route to the frame, attention, depth, light, or
sequence reference that solves the actual problem. Read only the references named
by that route.
