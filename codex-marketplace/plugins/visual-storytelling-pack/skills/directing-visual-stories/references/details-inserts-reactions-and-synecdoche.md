# Details, inserts, reactions, and visual synecdoche

A detail earns its crop by carrying evidence that the wider frame cannot carry as
clearly. A hand can stand for labour, intent, hesitation, or identity; an eye can
carry attention, fear, recognition, or point of view; a worn surface can expose
history; a tool or document can change the causal reading. This is visual
synecdoche: a part makes a larger person, action, or world present.

## Select the detail by consequence

Use a close crop or insert when it:

- reveals the cause of an action or the evidence of a prior action;
- transfers point of view or makes a reaction legible;
- delays identity while preserving a clue;
- establishes a recurring motif or foreshadows a change;
- punctuates a larger field with tactile scale;
- shows consequence that spectacle or dialogue would leave abstract.

An insert can be a photograph, shot, comic panel, inset over a matte, UI region,
tile exception, or interactive inspection state. It may change time, magnify
evidence, show simultaneity, or provide a subjective fragment. State its relation
to the containing field and what read it interrupts or completes.

## Reaction is part of the beat

The audience often understands an event through the face, body, silence, or changed
distance of the witness. Pair action and reaction when the meaning is relational.
Choose whether the reaction is immediate, delayed, withheld, or shown before the
cause. A reaction frame can be the final read even when the action is visually
larger.

## Guard against ornamental coverage

Do not ask for “dramatic eyes,” “a hand close-up,” or “detail shots” without naming
what interpretation depends on the detail. Preserve identity, crop, lighting,
silhouette, and spatial cause when an insert is generated as part of a larger
image. If removing the detail would not change knowledge, feeling, anticipation,
or action, it is decoration and should be cut.

## Brief format

`Detail: <part>; stands for <larger evidence>; audience learns/feels <change>; relation to containing field <...>; before/after beat <...>; preserve <invariants>.`
