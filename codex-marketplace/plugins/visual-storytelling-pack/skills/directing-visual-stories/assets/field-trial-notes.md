# Directing Visual Stories — field-trial note

This is a real retrospective trial against the Silk continuation. It records the
accepted work rather than inventing a baseline or claiming a new human decision.

## Trial

- Date: 2026-09-16
- Work item / route: Portfolio draft PR #61, `codex/port-17-silk-continuation`
- Medium and deliverable: Usual Specialists V2 page field; Index-to-Silk and
  Opening-to-Index crossing lockups; Commission 05 landscape and portrait frames
- Reviewer: Harley's acceptance is recorded in PR #61; this note is an agent
  field-use record, not a replacement for that review.

## Story problem

- Problem: make a chapter transition feel like one physical authored world while
  keeping the Silk aperture readable across wide, compact, and portrait states.
- Audience change: notice the rope/lock crossing as a consequential handoff,
  understand that Index and Silk are connected, and anticipate the next chapter.
- Story beat or state: a stable page boundary is breached by a mineral lockup;
  the accepted Commission 05 frames make the aperture a place rather than a card.
- Scale: `complete field` containing subordinate `frame` and detail beats
- Viewing contract: `directed`

## Direction

- Primary compositional family: internal frame and occlusion, with a physical
  crossing as the attention vector.
- Supporting families: depth-plane staging; full-field/page rhythm.
- Crop and scale: preserve the authored landscape and intentionally taller
  portrait proportions; use a heavy aperture rim to hide the rectangular world
  viewport boundary.
- Viewpoint and perspective: parent connector owns placement, scale, rotation,
  and z-order while the lockup keeps its intrinsic internal composition.
- Depth, focus, and attention path: foreground rope/rim → crossing lock → Silk
  world behind the aperture; the hidden edge makes the reveal legible.
- Light and palette: mineral wall and dark aperture contrast separate world from
  page plane; no new palette claim is inferred here.
- Motion or stillness: accepted parallax remains bounded and reduced-motion-safe.
- Off-frame implication / surrounding field: the connector implies a chapter beyond
  the visible edge and keeps the next section physically near, not floating.
- Invariant to preserve downstream: both crossings remain instances of one
  reusable lockup; chapter children stay opaque; the viewport edge stays hidden.
- Avoid list: thin rim treatment that exposes a straight crop edge; caller styling
  reach-in; treating the crossing as decorative card chrome.

## Evidence and learning

- Accepted decisions: PR #61 records acceptance of the mineral-aligned Commission
  05 landscape/portrait frames, heavy aperture rim, reusable connector ownership,
  and bounded parallax/reduced-motion behaviour.
- Rejected decisions and why: the regular thin aperture candidate was rejected for
  the locked rectangular viewport because its top edge could not fully hide the
  boundary; its candidate custody remains comparison evidence.
- What survived translation: the story relationship, rope topology, hidden edge,
  responsive authored states, and source/derivative custody all remain explicit in
  code, tests, and PR evidence.
- Friction or ambiguity: the existing work contained strong composition decisions
  but no single labelled frame/page direction. The new skill makes the audience
  change, containing field, invariant, and downstream image-generation boundary
  explicit without rewriting the accepted UI.
- Portable lesson (only if justified): a crossing frame is a story beat only when
  its containing field, hidden boundary, and next-state implication are specified;
  a visually attractive rim alone is insufficient.
- Follow-up: use this contract prospectively for the next Specialists visual slice;
  keep Plan 4 marketplace custody separate.
