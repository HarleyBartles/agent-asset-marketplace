# Sneak peek boundary

A sneak peek is participant-facing character-read preview text. It is separate from a participant memory prompt sheet.

## Purpose

Let a participant see an appropriate version of the project's current read of them so they can say what feels
right, wrong, too neat, missing, overdramatic, or not really them.

## Canonical style

Use this style by default unless your human partner explicitly asks for a different form:

- start with a short intro only;
- avoid writerly framing such as "as a writer," "what I am trying to do," or long explanation of the book's mechanics;
- follow the intro with a series of direct `[Name] is ...` statements;
- make each statement compact, plain, and participant-facing;
- end with concise correction questions.

The canonical correction questions are:

- What feels like you?
- What feels off?
- What is too neat?
- What is missing?
- What is true but phrased wrong?
- What would someone misunderstand about you from this version?

## Statement guidance

Good sneak-peek statements describe the current character read at participant-safe level. They may cover:

- public voice;
- private or room register;
- social posture;
- boundaries;
- relationship texture;
- visible recurring modes or bits;
- places where the read may be too neat or incomplete.

Keep the statements readable and concrete. Prefer "Mel is funny without seeming desperate to be funny" over
abstract explanation.

Do not make the statement series a questionnaire. Do not ask for memories inside the statement series.

## Do not let sneak peek kill the prompt sheet

Do not ask full memory-prompt questions in a sneak peek. Do not exhaust rooms, DMs, public exchanges, room
lineages, conflicts, or event memories. Leave those for rooms-sheet-creator.

A sneak peek may mention that separate memory prompts may follow or exist, but it should not merge the two processes.

## Participant-facing safety

Default to:

- plain language;
- no repo jargon;
- no archive/transcript case-file posture;
- no hidden structural/thematic interpretation unless your human partner explicitly approves disclosure;
- no direct transcript quotes unless explicitly approved;
- no claim that the participant must confirm the project's read.

## Good and bad moves

Good moves:

- "[Name] is ..." statements.
- "This is not definitive."
- "What feels off?"
- "What is true but phrased wrong?"
- "What would someone misunderstand about you from this version?"

Bad moves:

- Confirm that you were the stable mirror to X.
- Explain your function in the book.
- Verify this transcript/account of what happened.
- Tell us all the rooms/events this reminds you of.
- Here is the archive evidence for how we read you.
- Here is the prompt sheet in miniature.

## Relation to rooms-sheet-creator

The sneak peek can inform later prompt-sheet sensitivity, but it should not replace the prompt sheet. rooms-sheet-creator decides memory prompts separately and should not assume the participant has seen or accepted the
sneak peek unless your human partner says so.
