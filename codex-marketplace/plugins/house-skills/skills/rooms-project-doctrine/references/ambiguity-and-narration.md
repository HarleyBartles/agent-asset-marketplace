# Ambiguity and narration discipline

Use `rooms-ambiguity-buster-v1` when identity, motive, authorship, witness status, archive gaps, room history, narrator knowledge, reconstruction, disappearance, or emotional cost could be overresolved.

Preserve questions such as:

- who experienced;
- who witnessed;
- who reconstructed;
- who narrated;
- who benefited;
- who exposed;
- who protected;
- who disappeared;
- who paid the emotional cost.

`We do not know` is a valid Rooms answer. Do not say `most likely` without provenance. When evidence is partial, say what is known, what is unknown, what route was checked, and what would be needed to resolve it.

No omniscient narrator is a project posture. Do not let GPT produce all-knowing certainty where the artifacts, narrator, archive, or manuscript do not support it.
