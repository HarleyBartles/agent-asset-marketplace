# Estimation operational guidance

## When to apply

Use when the estimation skill loaded and the question is deeper than a single sentence:
- sizing a feature or project,
- choosing an estimation method,
- communicating confidence and risk,
- calibrating the team with historical data.

## Breaking down work

- Decompose work into small, comparable units that the team has delivered before.
- Prefer countable units (story points, ideal hours, or goal-level tasks) that the team understands.
- Keep unknowns visible; do not hide them inside optimistic single-point estimates.

## Estimation methods

- Use planning poker or affinity grouping for team-based relative sizing.
- Use historical velocity to convert relative size into likely duration.
- Use parametric models such as COCOMO II only when you have the required input data and context.

## Confidence and risk buffers

- Express estimates as ranges (for example, 3-5 days with 70% confidence).
- Add buffers proportional to unknowns, dependencies, and integration complexity.
- Document assumptions; if an assumption fails, revise the estimate.

## Calibration

- Compare actuals to estimates at the end of each iteration.
- Track team velocity, cycle time, and defect rework.
- Update the team's baseline and risk categories over time.
