# Research Ops operational guidance

## When to apply

Use when the research-ops skill is loaded and the task involves planning research, triaging sources, synthesizing evidence, or managing citations.

## Evidence-first workflow

1. Start with a concrete question and inclusion criteria; write them down before searching.
2. Search broadly, then screen sources against the criteria and record exclusions.
3. Extract claims and evidence into an annotated bibliography or synthesis matrix.

## Source triage

1. Distinguish primary sources from commentary; prefer peer-reviewed or official releases.
2. Record source URLs, access dates, and licenses at the point of capture.
3. Flag stale, paywalled, or unverifiable sources before relying on them.

## Synthesis and citation

1. Synthesize by theme or claim, not by source count.
2. Ground every conclusion in a cited source; note confidence and limitations.
3. Use a consistent citation style and keep the bibliography machine-readable.

## Common mistakes

- Searching without inclusion criteria. → Write the research question and criteria first.
- Copying claims without recording the source. → Capture source, access date, and quote in one step.
- Treating synthesis as a summary list. → Group by ideas and compare evidence.
